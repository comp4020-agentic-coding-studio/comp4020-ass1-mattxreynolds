not this file.

## Thesis

Looking further out in space is not "more distant stuff" — it's automatically
looking further back in time, because light takes time to arrive. Most people
know that in the abstract but don't feel it. The point this piece has to land:
**the observable universe has an edge in time, not in space.** The Cosmic
Microwave Background isn't "the furthest galaxy we've found so far, expect
more later" — it's a hard wall. Before recombination (~380,000 years after the
Big Bang) the universe was an opaque plasma; there is no light from earlier to
ever find, no matter how good telescopes get. That wall, reached and felt as a
wall (not read as a caption), is the piece's one job.

## Core interaction

One sticky, full-viewport stage sits inside a tall scroll track. Scroll
position maps to a single continuous `progress` value (0–1); the page never
visibly pans — it zooms. Every object is an independent layer stacked in that
stage, each with its own `{progress, scale, x, y, opacity}` keyframes,
interpolated continuously as `progress` changes, generated by
`schedule.ts`'s `buildSchedule()` from named phase durations
(`DURATIONS`/`GAPS` in `site-schedule.ts`) rather than hand-picked per
waypoint. There is no scroll-jacking: it's a plain native `scroll` listener on
a tall track, so the scrollbar, keyboard, and wheel all behave normally — the
transform is the only thing that's synthetic. Resizing the viewport preserves
scroll progress rather than an absolute pixel offset.

Two entrance grammars, chosen per transition, not applied uniformly:

- **Sibling body** (Moon → Sun → Proxima …): the incoming object starts
  oversized and offset to one side, invisible; fades in while still huge and
  off-centre; then scale and position converge toward centred/1× together
  while the outgoing object's own keyframes independently shrink it to a dot
  and fade it out. The offset magnitude is a unit direction in
  `schedule.ts`, scaled at render time in `main.ts` by that waypoint's own
  rendered image size and the current viewport, so every waypoint shows the
  same small sliver of itself at entrance regardless of size class or
  viewport width.
- **Field reveal** (single body → swarm/structure, e.g. a star → the galaxy
  it's one of many in): the incoming layer starts at a much larger scale with
  no lateral offset — it fades in centred, directly ahead, then shrinks as a
  whole. This is the "that was just one of these" beat. Used at the two
  category jumps: star-field-as-galaxy (Sagittarius A*) and
  galaxy-as-cluster (Virgo Cluster).

The reionization fog and the CMB (the two narrative-bridge waypoints that
aren't bodies in the same sense as the rest) use the same
`LAYER_MARKUP`/`LAYER_FRAMES` machinery and the same `<img>` markup as every
other waypoint. Each gets a scoped CSS size override instead: the fog is
full-bleed (`width/height: 100%`, `object-fit: cover`, no drop-shadow — there's
no edge to cast one from), and the CMB renders much larger than the default
waypoint clamp.

JADES → fog and fog → CMB use the same exit-shrink/fade-in durations and the
same small `GAPS.sibling` overlap as every other waypoint handoff, so the
transition reads the same size as any sibling cut. The CMB holds at full
opacity, then fades back out before the track ends — the ending is the
closing text alone on the ordinary dark background, not the text permanently
overlaid on a wall that never leaves. The closing layer's own fade-in starts
before the CMB has fully gone, so there's overlap rather than a hard cut to
black.

**Fog/CMB imagery:** `reionization-fog.png` and `cmb.png` — the latter is the
real Planck all-sky temperature-anisotropy map, used literally rather than a
stylised blackbody-glow treatment.

A fixed HUD overlay shows discrete state — name, distance, lookback label,
anchor — for whichever waypoint's `from` progress-threshold the current
`progress` has most recently crossed. That discrete mapping (progress →
current waypoint) is what the spec test asserts against, not the raw
transform math. On desktop, waypoints with diegetic callout cards (see below)
replace the HUD entirely rather than showing alongside it.

`title-layer` and `closing-layer` are schedule slots like any waypoint (driven
by `LAYER_FRAMES.title`/`.closing` via `interpLayer`), just with no image and
no distance/lookback data, so they sit outside the `staged`/HUD/ruler/callout
machinery entirely. The title holds at a large resting scale over the ambient
starfield, then scales *up* while fading out — deliberately the one exit that
doesn't shrink like every other waypoint's, since the title is framed as
nearer than everything else, so its exit reads as the camera pushing through
it rather than it receding. The Moon's own entrance starts well into that fade
(~85% through), not concurrently with it. The closing layer mirrors this in
reverse after the CMB: starts oversized and fades in while shrinking to its
settled scale, arriving once the CMB has almost fully faded back out (~85%
through its own fade-out) so there's a brief near-black beat before the text
appears, rather than the two crossfading for their full length.

## Desktop info cards (all 12 waypoints)

Each waypoint's diegetic callout is two cards:

- **Measurement card**: name, distance, lookback label, always-visible "In
  human terms" relatable analogy. Positioned with a fixed per-waypoint offset
  (`CARD_OFFSETS` in `main.ts`), tuned per waypoint rather than a shared
  constant — a single reusable offset doesn't survive contact with each
  waypoint's own entrance sweep, so each sibling-body waypoint's card sits on
  the side **opposite** its own entrance-sweep sign (dodging its own oversized
  entrance), with the vertical sign alternating between every adjacent pair
  (so two neighbours can safely share an x-side without their cards colliding
  mid-crossfade). The two field-reveal waypoints (Sagittarius A*, Virgo
  Cluster) have no lateral entrance to dodge, so their side just continues the
  left/right alternation implied by neighbours, with a larger offset
  magnitude to clear their bigger peak oversized scale. Fog and CMB have no
  discrete object or entrance sweep to dodge — both are full-bleed/large
  backdrops frozen at stage-centre — so their offsets are chosen directly from
  the rendered image instead (clear of each image's brightest/busiest region).
- **Identity card**: one factual "what is this" line (`Waypoint.whatIsIt`),
  as a `position: fixed` tooltip that appears at the cursor on hover
  (`wireIdentityHover` in `main.ts`), edge-aware so it never overflows the
  viewport. No leader line, since it appears where the cursor already is.
  Force-hidden if the waypoint's opacity drops below the visibility
  threshold, so it can't get stuck open while scrolling past without moving
  the mouse. Needs no positional offset — it isn't part of the measurement
  card's stacked-offset geometry.

Data for all 12 waypoints lives in `waypoints.ts` (`whatIsIt` field); all 12
are wired into `CARD_OFFSETS` in `main.ts`.

**Anchor fact: static, not click-gated.** The anchor line is always visible
on every card, labelled "In human terms" — no click-to-reveal gate, since it's
the emotional payoff of the whole card and gating it risked people never
seeing it.

## Mobile (`<768px`)

A bottom-sheet HUD (`.hud`) replaces the desktop callouts: name, distance,
lookback, and the anchor fact (same "In human terms" label as desktop, also
always-visible, no click-gate) sit permanently expanded in one full-width
card that sinks into / grows from the bottom edge as its waypoint's own
opacity rises and falls. A separate small floating identity card
(`.identity-mobile`, `MOBILE_IDENTITY_OFFSETS` in `main.ts`) shows the "what
is this" line, attached to the object's own live transform so it enters from
the same side and grows/shrinks with it. The ruler is a horizontal bar under
the header instead of desktop's vertical strip, reclaiming vertical space on
a narrow/tall viewport.

## Ruler

Ships as a bare segment bar with no text labels. **Progress dimming**
(already-scrolled-past segments dim, current segment brightens, everything
ahead stays at baseline) is what carries "how much further" — brightness
alone, not text. Corner-rounding lives on the end segments, not a clipping
parent, so nothing clips if content ever needs to render outside the bar's
own box.

The track ends once the last waypoint (the CMB) has settled and faded back
out, then the closing layer is the last thing on screen — no hard scroll-stop,
`progress` just clamps at 1.

## Scope

- **10–12 waypoints**, ordered nearest → CMB. No locked "must-have" subset —
  decided deliberately: the safety net against running out of time is build
  **order** (thesis-critical waypoints — near objects, the first cosmological
  jump, the CMB wall — built first), not a formal cut line.
- **Every waypoint carries a historical/human-scale anchor**, and every anchor
  is fact-checked before it ships. An anchor that doesn't check out gets
  replaced or dropped, not shipped as a guess.
- The escalation itself is part of the argument: early anchors are ordinary
  ("since breakfast," "since you were born"); by the later waypoints, no
  human-scale anchor is left ("before Earth existed," "before any star
  existed") — that collapse of the anchor device is the run-up to the CMB
  wall, not a separate decoration.

## Explicitly excluded

- No redshift formula, no explanation of *how* lookback time is derived —
  only the result (distance and time diverge; the CMB is a hard boundary).
- No second dial/toggle beyond distance-vs-time (e.g. no separate "speed of
  light" playback mode) — the divergence between the two labels at each
  waypoint carries that idea without a second mechanic.
- No attempt at statistical/scientific rigor beyond getting each stated
  number right — this is an explainer, not a research artefact.
